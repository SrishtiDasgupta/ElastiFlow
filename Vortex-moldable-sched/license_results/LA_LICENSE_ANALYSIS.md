# License-Aware Scheduling — figure analysis & the balanced view

Prose companion to the LA license-analysis figure suite in
`license_results/plots/` (the `LA_*` and `XWORKLOAD_*` figures, regenerated by
`generate_license_plots.py`). Every figure gets: **what it shows → what we
conclude → the caveat.** The closing section states the balanced license view
the chapter argues.

## Scope, data & terminology

- **Dataset.** All `LA_*` numbers come from `canonical_results.json` — 5 policies
  × N ∈ {150, 200, 300, 400, 500, 600, 700} × 6 seeds = 210 cells, baseline
  33/33/34 solver deck, cost-depth allocation. Boundary/scarcity figures read
  `data_la_boundary.json` / `data_la_scarcity.json`. Cross-workload figure draws
  on Plain (`plain_results_per_run.json`) and HPO (`SUMMARY_4CORNER.md`).
- **Policies** (display names; `policy_names.py` is the single source of truth):
  STATIC-FCFS, STATIC-EDF (static baselines); ELASTIC-FCFS (LAMF), ELASTIC-EDF
  (EDF-LAMF), ELASTIC-HSM (EDF-HSM). Terminology is **Static / Elastic**, never
  "moldable".
- **Currency** USD = EUR × 1.10. **Cost model is fixed** (cited Henkel & Treiber
  2015 token laws: LS-Dyna linear, ABAQUS concave, ANSYS workgroup); only pool
  capacity and workload are varied.
- **Headline N = 300** for snapshot (bar) figures — mid-load, representative of
  the license-bound regime; vs-N figures show the full N sweep.

## Group 1 — Cost structure: licenses are the binding tier

**`LA_01` cost structure (stacked hardware + license, N=300).**
Shows: per-workflow cost split into compute and token tiers. License tokens are
**68–70 % of total cost** for every policy. Totals: STATIC-FCFS $152, STATIC-EDF
$131, ELASTIC-FCFS $145, ELASTIC-EDF $131, ELASTIC-HSM $133.
Conclude: the binding tier is **licenses, not hardware**, and total cost is
essentially flat across the static/elastic divide (the EDF pair is identical at
$131). Elasticity moves compute, but compute is the minority tier here.
Caveat: a snapshot at one N; the vs-N figures confirm the pattern holds.

**`LA_02` license intensity (license ÷ hardware cost %, vs N).**
Shows: the cost ratio climbs from ~270 % (N=150) to 370–440 % (N=700) — licenses
already cost 2.6–4.4× hardware and the gap widens with load. ELASTIC-EDF/HSM end
highest (440 %), the FCFS pair lowest (~370 %).
Conclude: load makes the regime *more* license-dominated, so any compute-side
lever matters less as N grows.
Caveat: within each family static and elastic track each other — elasticity does
not change the license-vs-hardware split.

**`LA_03` license cost per completed workflow (USD, vs N).**
Shows: separates by **ordering**, not by static/elastic. The FCFS family rises to
~$120 by N=700; the EDF family + HSM stay ~$83.
Conclude: deadline-driven (EDF) ordering spends ~30 % less license money per
*successful* workflow under load — it avoids pouring tokens into work that will
miss.

## Group 2 — Efficiency & waste: the one genuine elastic edge

**`LA_04` effective license utilisation (% of budget spent on completed work, vs N).**
Shows: at **N=150 ELASTIC-HSM 80.0 % and ELASTIC-EDF 79.3 % vs STATIC-EDF
69.0 %** (+11 pp); at N=200 still ~+5–8 pp. The advantage **crosses to a loss**
by N=300 and worsens to **−6.5 pp at N=700** (ELASTIC-EDF/HSM 67 % vs STATIC-FCFS
78 %).
Conclude: this is the chapter's **one defensible elastic license win** — at
low-to-moderate load the elastic EDF variants direct a larger share of the
expensive token budget to work that completes, via fail-fast preemptive token
reclamation (DDM-EDF abandons doomed work early, so a miss wastes fewer tokens).
Caveat: it is a **narrow N=150–200 band**, bands overlap across seeds, and it
**reverses under saturation** — present as "low-load advantage", never a blanket
win.

**`LA_05` wasted license cost per workflow (USD, vs N).**
Shows: the dollar magnitude of tokens spent on incomplete work (replaces a
redundant waste-% plot, which was exactly 100 − `LA_04`). The FCFS family wastes
the fewest dollars at high N (~$17–19) vs EDF-class (~$24–29).
Conclude: FCFS wastes fewer *dollars* partly by completing a cheaper solver mix
— read together with `LA_07`/`LA_08b`, not alone.

**`LA_06` token-seconds per completed workflow (millions, vs N).**
Shows: the physical twin of `LA_03`. FCFS family ~2.8–3.0 M; EDF + HSM ~2.2–2.4 M.
Conclude: EDF-class consumes ~20 % less license-*time* per success — again an
**ordering** effect, not an elasticity effect.

## Group 3 — Per-solver & pool: cause and effect of fairness

**`LA_08b` per-solver license-cost share (%, N=300) — the cause.**
Shows: **ANSYS is ~47–50 % of the license budget** while being only ~31 % of the
jobs; LSDYNA ~30–33 %, ABAQUS ~20 %. Flat across all policies — it is a
*workload* property, not a scheduling choice.
Conclude: ANSYS jobs are the token-hogs, so they are the ones a naive order can
strand.

**`LA_07` per-solver completion rate (%, N=300) — the effect.**
Shows: FCFS-class depresses **ANSYS to ~68–71 %** while ABAQUS/LSDYNA sit higher;
all three **EDF-class policies balance every solver at ~85–92 %** (STATIC-EDF:
ANSYS 90.7, ABAQUS 92.1, LSDYNA 90.7).
Conclude: FCFS ordering **starves the heaviest-token solver** — ANSYS jobs queue
behind cheaper work. EDF's deadline pressure rescues them. Static vs elastic
within each ordering class is identical → **ordering drives fairness, not
elasticity.**

**`LA_08` per-pool active utilisation (%, N=300) — the counterpoint.**
Shows: every pool sits at ~29–35 % across all policies; ANSYS is consistently the
*least*-utilised pool.
Conclude: **no pool is saturated** in active-window terms — the bottleneck is
*ordering/completion*, not raw token capacity. (Honest but visually flat; it
exists to rule out the "ran out of tokens" explanation.)

## Group 4 — Boundaries: where elastic reaches parity

**`LA_boundary` — Abaqus-concave parity map (ELASTIC-HSM − STATIC-EDF Δcost %).**
Shows: a **parity pocket only at N=300 with a slack ABAQUS pool** (×4: −1.5 %,
×2: −0.5 %). Everywhere else elastic is worse: +4.8 to +8.9 % at N=400 even with
slack tokens, +11 to +22 % once the pool binds (hatched cells).
Conclude: elastic cost-parity exists **only** where the constraint is soft
(slack *or* the concave ABAQUS law) **and** load is low-moderate. Concurrency
re-binds the token-hold overhead beyond that narrow pocket.

**`LA_scarcity` — token-budget trade-off (cost | miss | wait vs ×budget, EDF family, N=400).**
Shows: tightening the global token budget ×1→×0.5 **lowers cost and deadline
misses** (e.g. STATIC-EDF $113→$88, miss .112→.033) but **raises queue waits**
(~17k→24k s). Static EDF dominates the elastic variants at every budget level.
Conclude: scarcity throttles admission → less compute contention and on-demand
bursting → cheaper, fewer misses — **paid for in wait time** (not a free lunch).
Caveat: the scale is **global** (deduced: halving the pool drops *total* cost
22 %, impossible if it only touched ABAQUS at ~20 % of spend).

## The balanced license view

The honest, defensible position for the LA chapter — neither "elastic is
superior" nor a negative result, but a conditional, mechanism-grounded claim:

1. **The binding tier is licenses, not hardware** (`LA_01`, ~70 % of cost), and
   the regime gets *more* license-bound with load (`LA_02`). Elasticity's lever
   is the compute tier, so it has little room to help on total cost.

2. **In the license-bound regime, static EDF dominates** on cost and deadlines.
   No elastic policy is reliably cheaper (`LA_01`: EDF pair tied at $131;
   `LA_boundary`: parity only in one N=300 slack pocket), and elastic EDF
   *regresses* deadline misses.

3. **The lever that actually moves token economics is the ordering policy
   (FCFS vs EDF), not static-vs-elastic.** EDF spends ~30 % less per completed
   workflow (`LA_03`/`LA_06`) and balances all three solvers (`LA_07`), because
   ANSYS hogs ~half the token budget (`LA_08b`) and only deadline pressure keeps
   its heavy jobs from starving. Static and elastic behave identically *within*
   each ordering class.

4. **Elasticity's one genuine license win is effective utilisation at low load**
   (`LA_04`): +11 pp at N=150 (80 % vs 69 % of budget on completed work) via
   fail-fast token reclamation. It is real and mechanism-explained, but **narrow
   (N=150–200) and it reverses under saturation** (−6.5 pp at N=700), traded
   against ~2 pp more deadline misses.

5. **This is exactly the boundary the moldable-scheduling thesis predicts.**
   Elastic wins where the binding constraint is soft — hardware-dominated (HPO
   −70 % cost, Plain −41 % CPR) or the concave-ABAQUS / slack-pool /low-load
   pocket. Where licenses bind hard, the elastic mechanism fights a token
   constraint it cannot relax, and static EDF is the right tool. The cross-
   workload gradient (`XWORKLOAD`) is the single picture of this conditional
   advantage.

**One-line claim.** *In the license-bound regime, static EDF dominates on cost
and deadlines; elasticity's defensible contribution is a low-load effective-
utilisation gain (+~11 pp at N≈150 via fail-fast token reclamation), and the
real economic lever is deadline-aware ordering, not elastic allocation.*
